DISPATCH · 04
Oil & Gas · Global Operations · 100+ Systems

“Finance and controlling master data across 100+ SAP clients. No central source of truth. IDOC errors on every sync attempt.”

A global oil major ran finance and controlling master data across more than a hundred SAP clients with no authoritative source, and every synchronisation attempt drowned in IDOC errors. We built a distribution layer using IDOC classification and filter logic that routed only the right records to the right systems, plus an automated monitoring grid covering all of them. What used to take a team of administrators the entire week became a daily exception list of a handful of items. The model was rolled out as the global standard for FI/CO master data movement.

DISPATCH · 05
Pharma · Global SRM · 20,000 Users · 20 Countries

“FX rates drifting between 20 country ERPs and the central SRM hub. PO valuations breaking every month-end.”

Twenty country ERPs were drifting away from the central SRM hub on FX rates, breaking purchase order valuations every month-end. We designed a reverse-alignment process that pulled rates from each ERP, reconciled them centrally, and pushed back a single corrected set on a controlled cadence. Month-end PO disputes dropped to near zero across all 20 countries. The mechanism was made a standing global control in the SRM operating model.

Travel · Switzerland · Payments · Strategic Advisory

“Five payment providers across multiple booking channels, each integrated separately. No consolidated cash view.”

A Swiss travel group was juggling Ingenico, Computop, PayPal, Twint and SEPA across multiple booking channels, with every integration written as a standalone piece of work. The CFO received a single reconciled view of cash-in across all rails. The same architecture was extended into a blockchain advisory track to the CXO suite.

DISPATCH · 13
Multiple Sectors · S/4HANA Migration · Greenfield & Brownfield

“S/4HANA migration: the same three issues appear in almost every programme, regardless of sector or approach.”

Across more than ten S/4HANA programmes — greenfield implementations and brownfield system conversions alike, spanning manufacturing, pharma, financial services, and the public sector — the same points of risk appear before almost every cutover. Custom ABAP that behaves differently against the S/4HANA data model than it did in ECC, often without surfacing an error until a user triggers the right path in UAT. Integration endpoints that were correctly configured at the original go-live and have since drifted without anyone noticing. Functional scope that looks complete in design documentation but reveals gaps when real users open it in the new environment. None of these are unusual. All of them are identifiable earlier in the programme cycle with the right review approach — and easier to address before the cutover window than during it.
